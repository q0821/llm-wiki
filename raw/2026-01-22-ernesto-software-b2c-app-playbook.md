# My first app made $20,000 in the first 30 days of launching

作者：Ernesto Lopez（@ErnestoSOFTWARE）

My first app made $20,000 in the first 30 days of launching. I went from broke 19yo to retiring my mom and fiance at 21 in Miami. As I became successful in the SMMA space, I realized I was trading my time for money: sales calls, fulfilment, meetings, needy clients.

The reason I got into B2C apps is that apps are the only business that generates passive income: build it once, sell forever, subscription-based, no calls or meetings. I believe apps will make more millionaires than ecommerce over the next five years, but only a select few who act now will make it.

I launched my first app, Snapout, with zero experience. It made $20,000 in sales from two viral videos within 30 days. This was not luck but calculated moves. You can achieve similar results by following these eight steps.

## 1. App idea validation

Do not build a dream app nobody wants. Pick a niche and validate that consumers want it.

1. **Find a painful problem**：Find something people struggle with every day that an app could solve.
2. **Check the App Store**：If several apps in the niche make more than $10K/month, that is a positive signal.
3. **Download the top competitors**：Study the highest-revenue competitors, screenshot their onboarding and core loop, and study details.
4. **Check TikTok and Instagram**：Identify the dominant growth strategy. Usually it is influencers, faceless slideshows, UGC creators, or paid ads.

When competitors make money and creators actively make content for the niche, the author considers the idea validated and ready to build.

## 2. Build the app and tool stack

The author says MVP 1 is usually ugly and builds apps in 3 to 7 days using:

- Rork and Cursor for AI-assisted implementation.
- ChatGPT for prompts.
- Superwall for paywalls.
- Firebase for a database when needed.
- Pinterest and Dribbble for design references.
- Xcode for release.

Workflow:

1. **Gather reference images** from Pinterest, Dribbble, and competitors. Send them to ChatGPT with a design prompt, then give the resulting prompt and images to Rork or Cursor to recreate the design as a functional app.
2. **Copy validated onboarding structure and pricing, but keep the core app original**. The author says onboarding is 70% of the app because it converts downloads into paying users. The intended purpose is to remind users of their problem and establish the app as the solution. Add a hard, unskippable paywall at the end, asking for payment or a free trial. Use Superwall or RevenueCat, and initially use competitors' pricing and offers.
3. **Launch**. Obtain an Apple Developer membership ($99/year, claimed approval lead time 3 to 5 days). App Store listing needs a clear name, one strong subtitle, 3 to 5 screenshots, and a simple description. Subtitle and first screenshot matter most. Screenshots are a funnel: large text, one idea per screen, minimal UI. Clear but ugly screenshots outperform beautiful but confusing ones. The author links an App Store review-avoidance guide.

## 3. Marketing

The author frames marketing as the next bottleneck after the app is in the App Store: money follows attention. He proposes five channels.

### 3.1 UGC（User-Generated Content）

Hire creators to operate TikTok or Instagram accounts and post for the app. The author says UGC creators usually publish 30 to 60 posts per month. He claims to pay $15/video plus bonuses: $150 at 100K views, $300 at 250K, $600 at 500K, and $800 at 1M. Source creators through an agency/platform such as Sideshift or manually through TikTok. His personal recommendation is manual outreach. Always sign a contract and contact creators daily.

### 3.2 Influencers

The author says one good influencer can kick-start an app. He claims that a $1 CPM deal generated $20K for his first app from the first viral video in 30 days. Suggested guardrail: cap CPM at $1,000.

### 3.3 Faceless content

Free channels such as slideshows, screen recordings, text-on-screen, stock clips with captions, and app demos with hooks. Publish 3 to 5 times a day. When a format works, immediately repeat its hook and structure with different wording. Keep a consistent visual brand or character rather than looking random.

### 3.4 Founder-led content

The founder makes content directly. It is harder without content experience, but helps develop a sense for viral formats that can then be passed to creators. The author says this should be used even without budget for influencers.

### 3.5 Paid ads

The author calls paid ads the best growth channel because winning creatives and a good CPA（cost per acquisition）can scale predictably. He cites an investment in Prayer Lock and claims it reached $20K/month through four Spark Ad creatives made by a 15-year-old. Use Singular for mobile measurement and attribution across TikTok and Meta SDKs.

## Closing model

The author treats apps as digital real estate and users as tenants: repeat the formula, accumulate subscription income, and treat recurring income as the only real business model.
